Current Package Edition payload

The native APK set is the AGENTCODI engine and shell bridge, Community Codex
app-server and matching code-mode host, LLVM libc++ and zlib 1.3.2.
Node.js, npm, Python and ripgrep are supplied through the separate signed APT
catalog. Historical sandbox/tool attributions are retained in repository
NOTICE.md and do not enumerate this APK.

zlib: assets/third-party/zlib/ZLIB-LICENSE.
LLVM: assets/third-party/libcxx/DISTRIBUTOR-LICENSE and LLVM-LICENSES.
The exact libc++ distributor text is retained, supplemented with the complete
LLVM libc++/libc++abi/libunwind notices and LLVM exceptions.

Bootstrap: shell, APT, dpkg, CA certificates and runtime dependencies rebuilt
for /data/data/de.agentcodi.pkg/files/usr. The bootstrap keeps original license
files in share/doc, share/LICENSES and share/licenses. The legal-notices screen selects those
files from the bundled ZIP, using BOOTSTRAP-LICENSE-INDEX.json.
The package report records their ownership, sizes and SHA-256 values.
Corresponding sources, recipes, patches and edition build scripts accompany
the CI bootstrap artifact. Authenticated catalog source downloads:
https://mcpasi.github.io/AGENTCODI/apt/package-edition
See scripts/package-edition/README.md in the repository for reconstruction.

The legal screen selects individual Rust crate, Rust standard-library and V8
source notices. The collection uses the pinned aarch64-linux-android normal/build
Cargo dependency closure and exact V8 submodule revisions. Original texts and
supplied copyright notices are retained. When a publisher omits a standalone
legal file, the published crate's declared MIT/Apache terms and authors are
identified explicitly; no copyright date is invented. Additional V8 build/test
source notices are included conservatively and identified by their source path.

bzip2, gpgv and xz-utils include their parent package's original legal material
under their own dpkg ownership. The build verifies all indexed text hashes and
the native release binding. The report's license_release_ready describes legal
prerequisites; it separately records that physical Android tests remain open.
